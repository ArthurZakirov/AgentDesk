# ⏱️ Effort and scope checkpoints

For work governed by a ticket, specification, or recorded agreement, use its intended outcome, acceptance criteria, and documented effort expectation as the baseline. Include human understanding/review, testing, integration, and coordination in effort—not just agent code-generation time. Story points imply hours only when the team's convention establishes that mapping.

| When | Then |
| --- | --- |
| **Before substantial implementation:** Repository inspection reveals concrete cost drivers—such as missing reusable test infrastructure, changes across multiple systems, or unusually costly human review—and suggests the complete work may materially exceed the remaining agreed effort. | Check the controlling ticket and existing code/test patterns. Estimate a plausible range for *remaining* end-to-end effort, identify the work behind it, and compare it with the remaining budget. If a material overrun is likely, **pause that implementation before investing further** and present a compact scope checkpoint to Arthur: agreed outcome/budget, required extra work, estimated cost including review, and the specific decision needed from the accountable scope owner. Continue normally when evidence supports the budget; a large diff or uncertain estimate alone does not prove an overrun. |
| **During implementation or review:** Actual effort approaches or exceeds the budget without a reviewable outcome; the diff, affected components, tests, or dependencies expand materially; or repeated implementation/review cycles and requests for explanation or simplification indicate that human understanding is not stabilizing. | Reassess *remaining* effort and the technical approach using the actual diff, time spent, and review friction. Do not merely give another explanation or continue implementing by default. If the cost or approach has materially changed, **stop the expanding portion**, preserve work, and surface the revised cost and reason to Arthur for an accountable scope decision before resuming. One ordinary clarification, a large line count, or a difficult but already approved task is not itself a stop condition. |

For company/shared work, Arthur's instruction to continue does not by itself authorize unplanned scope or effort. The manager, accountable scope owner, or explicitly delegated decision-maker must confirm the trade-off; a technical expert may identify a cheaper existing approach but does not authorize scope without delegation. While awaiting a decision, do only independently useful, already authorized work that does not depend on the unanswered choice. For purely private experiments with no impact on work commitments or shared systems, Arthur can approve the effort and time box himself.
